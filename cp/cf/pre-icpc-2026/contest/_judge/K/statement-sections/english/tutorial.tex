Only paying creates zeros: the free operation can produce a zero only as $\max$ of two zeros. Thus, if the leftmost and rightmost positions you pay for are $i$ and $j$, every position between them can be zeroed for free, producing a zero segment of length $j - i + 1$. Paying for more than two positions never helps, since only the two extreme paid positions matter.

Therefore, for $k \ge 2$, the answer is the minimum of $a_i + a_j$ over all pairs with $j - i + 1 \ge k$. For $k = 1$, the answer is simply the minimum element of the array.

Scan $j$ from left to right while maintaining the minimum of $a_i$ over $i \le j - k + 1$; each step updates the answer with $\text{prefixMin} + a_j$. This gives an $O(n)$ solution per test case, $64$-bit integers are required since the answer can reach $2 \cdot 10^9$.
