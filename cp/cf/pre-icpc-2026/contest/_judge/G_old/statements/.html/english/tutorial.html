Self-Crossing Turtle

Extending move $$$i$$$, the turtle can only hit one of the three most recent parallel-or-perpendicular candidates: the segments drawn $$$3$$$, $$$4$$$ or $$$5$$$ moves ago.

Why? The direction rotates by $$$90^\circ$$$ after every move, so segment $$$i$$$ is parallel to segment $$$i - 2$$$ and perpendicular to segments $$$i - 1$$$ and $$$i - 3$$$. Segment $$$i - 1$$$ only shares the ordinary continuation point, and segment $$$i - 2$$$ runs on a different line. Segments $$$i - 6$$$ and older are already fenced off: to reach them, segment $$$i$$$ would first have to cross one of segments $$$i - 3$$$, $$$i - 4$$$ or $$$i - 5$$$, whose endpoints lie on the closed spiral built so far. So a crossing, if it ever happens, is detected within those three candidates.

Each candidate translates into one condition on the distances (all comparisons are inclusive, so touching counts):

1. Crossing segment $$$i - 3$$$ (for $$$i \ge 3$$$): $$$d_i \ge d_{i - 2}$$$ and $$$d_{i - 1} \le d_{i - 3}$$$. 2. Folding onto segment $$$i - 4$$$ (for $$$i \ge 4$$$): $$$d_{i - 1} = d_{i - 3}$$$ and $$$d_i + d_{i - 4} \ge d_{i - 2}$$$. 3. Reaching segment $$$i - 5$$$ (for $$$i \ge 5$$$): $$$d_{i - 2} \ge d_{i - 4}$$$ and $$$d_i + d_{i - 4} \ge d_{i - 2}$$$ and $$$d_{i - 1} \le d_{i - 3}$$$ and $$$d_{i - 1} + d_{i - 5} \ge d_{i - 3}$$$.

Scan the array once, keeping only the last five distances, and apply the three checks.

The complexity is $$$O(n)$$$ per test case and the memory usage is $$$O(1)$$$ besides storing the input. Beware: the straightforward check of all pairs of segments takes $$$O(n^2)$$$ and is too slow under these constraints.
